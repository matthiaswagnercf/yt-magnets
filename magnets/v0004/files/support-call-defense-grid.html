What a hardware key stops, and what it does not

  • Stops: move 3. Under Google's Advanced Protection you are required to sign in with a passkey or security key, and a passkey cannot be written down or accidentally given to a bad actor. The Drive stays shut, the Gemini records stay unseen.
  • Does not stop: moves 4, 5 and 6. Those calls never touch the Google account.
  • Does not stop: move 1. Nothing on this grid stops someone else triggering login alerts, and nothing needs to.

If you already took the call and granted access

  1. Hang up and call back on a number you find yourself. Google says it will never call you about your account security and never asks you to read a verification code or approve a device prompt over the phone. Gemini says it will never call or text its customers.
  2. End the remote session and remove the remote-access software. Gemini's page is explicit: never allow remote access into your computer.
  3. Recover the account that holds the records, then put a security key on it (Google Advanced Protection).
  4. Tell the exchange. Gemini applies a 72-hour hold on withdrawals after an account recovery, which buys time only if the coins sit in the exchange account.

What this cannot tell you

The memo says he opened files containing private keys, but not which wallet those keys opened. In a wallet of his own, no exchange control applied, and the 72-hour hold above would have changed nothing. The memo also says he was targeted because the group already believed he held crypto, so moves 4 to 6 might have played out even with a key. This grid is a floor, not a guarantee.
