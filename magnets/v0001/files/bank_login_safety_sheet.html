The Bank-Login Safety Sheet

Built for the Breach Record video on the web3adspanels.org seizure (DOJ, December 22, 2025). Free. No email required to read it; the sheet version is delivered by email.

The 6 checks before you type a bank password

#CheckWhy (from the record)
1Did you get here from an ad? If the result says "Sponsored" or "Ad", close it.The DOJ seizure: criminals bought Google and Bing ads that mimicked real bank ads; clicking sent victims to fake bank sites.
2Is the address the one on your bank card, letter for letter, before the first slash?Look-alike domains differ by one character; the FBI alert says to check the URL for typos or a misplaced letter.
3Did you type it, or use your bookmark?The FBI and the DOJ both recommend bookmarks or typing the address yourself, never a search result.
4Does your bank's login page ask for something it never asked before (full card number, PIN, a code before you asked for one)?Harvest pages copy the look and add fields the real site does not have.
5Is your second factor a code sent by text?Codes can be intercepted or replayed; where the bank offers an app prompt or a passkey, switch.
6Do you have transaction alerts on?In the seized case, funds were drained after the login; alerts are the last chance to see it the same day.

The 19-victim timeline as the DOJ states it

  1. Fraudulent ads mimicking legitimate bank ads placed on Google and Bing.
  2. Victim clicks, lands on a fake bank site controlled by criminals.
  3. Login entered; credentials harvested by software embedded in the fake site.
  4. Credentials used on the real bank site; accounts drained.
  5. Server on web3adspanels.org stored the credentials of thousands of victims; still active November 2025; domain seized December 2025.

Numbers: at least 19 identified victims, about $28 million attempted, about $14.6 million actually lost; over 5,100 IC3 complaints about bank account takeover since January 2025, over $262 million reported lost.

If you already typed your password on a page you now doubt

  1. Call the number on the back of your card (not a number from the page or the ad).
  2. Change the bank password from a bookmarked or typed address, then every account that shared it.
  3. Turn on alerts and, if offered, an app-based or passkey login.
  4. File at ic3.gov if money moved.

Sources: DOJ press release 2025-12-22 (justice.gov/opa); FBI IC3 PSA I-122122-PSA (2022-12-21).
