RCW 19.255.010(8): notice "in the most expedient time possible, without unreasonable delay, and no more than thirty calendar days after the breach was discovered." Whether an eight-month file review moves the discovery date is the Attorney General's question, not this page's.

This weekend

  1. Freeze your credit at all three bureaus. FTC: "There's no cost to place or lift a credit freeze"; while it is in place "nobody can open a new credit account in your name"; it "lasts until you lift it." The letter lists Equifax 1 (888) 298-0045, Experian 1 (888) 397-3742, TransUnion 1 (833) 799-5355.
  2. Or place a free one-year fraud alert with one bureau (letter p.6): lenders must verify you before opening credit.
  3. Enroll in the monitoring before November 12, 2026 with the code on your letter. It does not replace step 1.
  4. Pull the free reports at annualcreditreport.com and read your explanation-of-benefits statements; health insurance information was in the files.
  5. Keep the letter: the code, the hotline and the dates are your proof if a fraudulent account appears (identitytheft.gov).

What this cannot tell you

The letter never states a discovery date; day 269 uses the date Paylogix reported to Oregon. Oregon's "Number Affected" column is not labelled nationwide; the inference rests on its neighbours (Lennar Mortgage 348,416 in Oregon against 11,417 in Washington), which only a national figure explains. Nothing here says which of your fields were taken.
