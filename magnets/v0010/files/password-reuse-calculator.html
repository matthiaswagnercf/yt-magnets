Password Reuse Calculator

Built for the Breach Record video on the FTC complaint In the Matter of TaxSlayer, LLC, Docket No. C-4626, issued October 20, 2017 (the list-validation attack of October 10 to December 21, 2015). Free.

The formula

Extra accounts exposed per credential-stuffing hit = hit rate x k

  • hit rate = the share of accounts at the attacked site that fell to stolen passwords: 8,882 of 950,000+ = 0.935%, about 1 in 107 (TaxSlayer, 72 days, no multi-factor authentication, no rate limiting; complaint paras. 5, 15, 16, 17).
  • k = the number of your OTHER accounts that share the password of the account that gets hit. This is the only input you control.
  • "Trying it elsewhere" is the attack itself: "List validation attacks occur when remote attackers use lists of stolen login credentials to attempt to access accounts across a number of popular Internet sites, knowing that consumers often reuse user name and passwords combinations." (para. 15(c)(ii))

What moves the number

  • Yours: k. A password manager sets k to 0 for every account you move; 24% of adults used one in the survey.
  • Theirs: multi-factor authentication. TaxSlayer "implemented multi-factor authentication" on December 21, 2015, "on that day" the attack ended (para. 16).
  • Theirs: rate limiting. NIST SP 800-63B 5.2.2: "The verifier SHALL limit consecutive failed authentication attempts on a single account to no more than 100." TaxSlayer "failed to use readily-available tools to prevent devices or IP addresses from attempting to access an unlimited number of TaxSlayer Online accounts in rapid succession" (para. 15(c)(v)).
  • Theirs: breached-password checks. NIST 5.1.1.2: verifiers "SHALL compare the prospective secrets against a list that contains values known to be commonly-used, expected, or compromised." TaxSlayer's only rule: "eight to sixteen characters in length" (para. 15(c)(i)).
  • The events recur: New York's Attorney General reported 19,715 Dunkin' accounts compromised over five days in 2015 and "more than 300,000" by 2018; settlement $650,000 (Sept 15, 2020). No denominator is published, so Dunkin' gives events, not a rate.

One afternoon

  1. Install a password manager; move the accounts that hold money and mail first. k = 0 for each one moved.
  2. Check any password you keep at haveibeenpwned.com/Passwords: "only the first 5 characters of the SHA-1 hash are sent to the API"; the password stays on your machine.
  3. Turn on a second factor on the email account, the tax account and the bank.
  4. Count your accounts and write your own k in the table above.

Against that afternoon, paragraph 18: victims "had to call the IRS and state tax authorities, obtain PINs, file paper returns, wait months for refunds", and freeze and monitor credit.

What this cannot tell you

The 65% reuse figure is one survey, one country, one year. Thirty accounts and ten hits are assumptions; change them and the table scales. The hit rate is one site's, with no MFA and no rate limit; a site with both will show a lower one. Dunkin' has no published account base.
