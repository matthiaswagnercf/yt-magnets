If you ever paid Restoro or Reimage

  1. The refund finds you; you do not apply. Payments went to the email or address tied to the purchase.
  2. A PayPal payment must be redeemed within 30 days of the email.
  3. A mailed check must be cashed within 90 days.
  4. Questions go to the administrator on the FTC's page, Rust Consulting, 1-844-590-1102, not to a number that calls you.
  5. Any fee, "verification", gift card, bank login or crypto request to release the refund is a scam; see the lines below.

The refund-scam lines, in the FTC's words

  • "The Commission never requires people to pay money or provide account information to submit a claim or receive a refund." (press release, March 10, 2025)
  • "Never pay upfront for a refund or help with a refund." (Refund and Recovery Scams)
  • "Government agencies and legitimate organizations will never ask for money to help you get a refund. They will never ask for your financial account numbers or other personal information" (Refund and Recovery Scams)
  • "the FTC doesn't have 'agents.'" and "nobody who works at the FTC will ever, EVER tell you to move your money to protect it." (FTC Impersonators)

The scheme, in six lines from the complaint

  1. A pop-up scan on a computer the FTC describes as "free of performance and security issues" reported "552 issues" (Restoro, June 29, 2022) and "1,244 such 'issues'" (Reimage, August 3, 2022). (para. 22, 24)
  2. The software cost $27 to $58. (para. 25)
  3. The screen said to call a number to "activate" it; the call was the sales floor. (paras. 26-27)
  4. "The telemarketers first direct consumers to download a software that allows the telemarketers to gain remote access to consumers' computers." (para. 28)
  5. Windows' own event log was shown as damage; a virus scanner's results for a different computer were shown as infections. (paras. 29-34)
  6. "[W]ell, you know what Trojans can do... they can even gain back their access on your computer and steal some of your information." (para. 32, to an FTC investigator)

What this cannot tell you

No document states how many people were harmed or who lost the most; 736,375 is a count of payments. The average is arithmetic, not a figure any source states. The order's date line is garbled in the public PDF; March 18, 2024 comes from the FTC's case page. No individual is named in the complaint.
