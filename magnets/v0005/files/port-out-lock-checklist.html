Port-Out Lock Checklist

Built for the Breach Record video on the U.S. Attorney's Office (N.D. Texas) sentencing release in United States v. Bennett, No. 7:25-cr-00018, announced February 27, 2026. Free.

Read before you toggle:

  • Verizon: "Number Lock on your number doesn't help prevent SIM card changes or equipment changes to your device." Turn on both.
  • T-Mobile: only the Primary Account Holder can remove either feature; the 2024 page says SIM Protection "does not prevent eSIM transfer on Apple devices."
  • AT&T: each toggle emails the primary holder and texts every active line. Anyone with primary or secondary account access can flip it.
  • Another brand: ask by name for a number-transfer (port-out) PIN and an account lock. The rule below orders both at no cost.

2. The federal rule, in its own words

  • 47 CFR 64.2010(h)(4): "A CMRS provider shall offer customers, at no cost, the option to lock their accounts to prohibit the CMRS provider from processing requests to change the customer's SIM." 47 CFR 52.37(d) says the same for requests "to port the customer's number."
  • Adopted November 15, 2023; published December 8, 2023; effective January 8, 2024, except the lock and notification paragraphs, "delayed indefinitely" pending OMB review.
  • July 5, 2024, DA 24-649: the Wireline Competition Bureau waived compliance with the whole set until OMB finishes and a Federal Register notice sets the date. Carriers had asked for March 10, 2025.

What this cannot tell you

The release names no carrier and dates no swap, so it cannot say whether any victim had a lock available and left it off, or whether the thefts fell before or after January 8, 2024. The FCC notice setting the final compliance date for the lock paragraphs could not be opened; treat "in force today" as unverified.
